# 30-Day Challenge: 21 Posts, 3 Lanes

**Channel:** @samiam.in.tech
**Format:** Video only. No image posts, no news pegs.
**Cadence:** 5 posts per week, Monday to Friday. 21 posts across 30 days.
**Lanes:** A Consequence (AI x jobs x money), C Subway Build, D Receipts and Failures. Seven each.

Nothing here depends on a news cycle. Every concept is sourced from your own work, which means you can batch-capture, nothing expires, and a post written in week one still lands in week four.

---

## The signature open

Every video, all 21, uses the same three-part cold open. That repetition is the point. Viewers learn the shape, and a recognizable shape is what turns scattered posts into a channel.

**Beat 1. Artifact on screen, 0 to 1 second.**
No face. No "so." Open on the terminal, the error, the dashboard, the invoice, the train window. The first frame is roughly the eighth second of what you recorded.

**Beat 2. The claim, 1 to 4 seconds. Verbatim, memorized.**
One sentence. States the consequence up front. This is the only line you memorize and it carries the entire retention curve.

**Beat 3. The turn, 4 to 7 seconds.**
One sentence that opens the gap. Some variation of "here's what actually happened," "nobody wants to say why," "it didn't work."

Then the middle runs live and unscripted. Then a verbatim close.

### Fixed first-line grammar per lane

The claim changes every video. The grammar never does.

| Lane | Grammar | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Consequence** | [Concrete fact from your team]. [What it actually means]. | "We didn't backfill two engineers this quarter. That's not a freeze, that's the new baseline." |
| **C. Subway Build** | Subway Build [N]. [Time], [constraint], [what shipped]. | "Subway Build 12. 45 minutes, no wifi past 14th, one working onboarding flow." |
| **D. Receipts** | [Number]. [What it bought or cost]. | "$340 in tokens last month. One line item ate half of it." |

### Fixed close

Conviction, never a question. Then the same four-word tag every single time:

> **"Build the thing."**

A repeated sign-off is cheap continuity and it compounds. Do not vary it.

### On the numbers below

Every `[X]` is a slot. Fill it with a real number from your own logs, your own invoice, your own calendar. Do not invent one. Specificity is the entire credibility model on this channel, and a fabricated number is the one mistake that cannot be walked back.

# Lane A — Consequence (AI x jobs x money)

The highest-reach lane. Conviction reads as fake when it's performed, so these are the ones to shoot candid and shoot fast.

### A1. The backfill
**Claim:** "We didn't backfill the last two engineers. That's not a hiring freeze."
**Turn:** "Nobody on the leadership side wants to say the real reason out loud."
**Beats:** what the two roles actually did / what absorbed the work / what that means for the next req
**Close:** "The jobs aren't disappearing overnight. They're just not coming back after the next round. Build the thing."
**Caption keyword:** ai replacing engineering jobs

## Rules for the run

1. **Capture while doing.** Record during the work, not after. The reaction when something breaks is the asset.
2. **One memorized line per video.** The claim. Everything else is live.
3. **Cut the ramp-in.** Published frame one is roughly recorded second eight.
4. **Say the caption keyword out loud** in the first two seconds, put it on screen, and put it in the caption written the way people search.
5. **60 to 180 seconds.** Watch time is a core ranking signal and 25 seconds caps you.
6. **Same close every time.** "Build the thing."
7. **Never fabricate a number.** If you don't have the real one, change the concept.

## Measurement at day 30

Compare lanes on **median views and average completion**, not on the single best video. One breakout hides a weak lane.

Then: kill the weakest lane, double the strongest, keep Subway Build running underneath regardless of how it scores. The series is the spine, not a test variable.
