# Content Trend Research: Why "AI systems at work" Is Underperforming

**Date:** August 2026
**Channel:** @samiam.in.tech
**Question:** Recent videos on AI systems and how I use them at work are getting very low views. What trends perform better?

---

## 1. Diagnose before you pivot

Do not burn a topic that actually has a packaging problem. Pull retention on the last 6 videos and read the shape:

| What analytics show | Real problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Views die at 0 to 3 seconds | Hook. Topic may be fine. | Open on the consequence, not the setup |
| Impressions never grow past a few hundred | Demand. The topic has no cold audience. | Change topic lane |
| High completion, low views | Discovery. Content is good, nobody finds it. | Search-shaped titles and captions |
| Flat everything | Format fatigue | New format, same knowledge |

If it is the second or third row, the rest of this doc applies.

---

## 2. Why this specific topic is structurally capped

Three problems, none of them about video quality.

**It is middle-of-funnel content in a top-of-funnel format.** "How I use AI at work" only pays off for someone who already has the tool, already has the problem, and already cares. Short-form distributes to cold audiences first. The ceiling is set by the addressable audience of the topic, not the quality of the edit.

**No stakes.** A workflow walkthrough has no outcome, no number, no antagonist, no before and after. The viewer can predict the entire video from the first two seconds, so there is nothing to stay for. Retention collapses and the algorithm reads that as a weak video.

**Maximum competition, zero differentiation.** Every consultant, SaaS marketer, and career-advice account is shipping this exact video. Roughly 20% of what gets served to new YouTube users is now classed as AI slop, and TikTok's 2026 ranking explicitly filters repetitive and derivative content. Generic AI workflow content is the single most-produced category on the internet right now.

The topic is not dead. The framing is.

---

## 3. Trends that are actually working, ranked for this channel

### A. Consequence content: AI x jobs x money

The highest-reach tech topic on short-form right now. More than 175,000 tech layoffs in 2026 as of August, already past 2025's full-year total, with Google, Zillow, Etsy and TikTok cutting in the first week of August alone. Meanwhile companies are opening premium AI comp bands, ClickUp went public with million-dollar bands for people driving outsized impact with AI.

Why it fits: the audience for "what does AI mean for my job and my salary" is 50x the audience for "how do I configure an MCP server." This is already pillar 4 in the voice profile, and the strongest sample lines in that doc live here ("Token budget is replacing headcount. Nobody wants to say it out loud."). Underused lane.

### B. Search-intent utility

TikTok now behaves like a search engine. 49% of US consumers use it as one, 65% among Gen Z. Watch time and completion are core FYP ranking factors, and captions now matter more than hashtags for discovery.

Why it fits: same knowledge, query-shaped packaging. "How I use AI at work" is not a query anyone types. "claude code pricing," "what is MCP," "AI tools for product managers," "is vibe coding real" all are. Search-shaped videos accumulate views for months instead of dying in 48 hours.

Mechanics that matter: say the target keyword out loud in the first 2 seconds, put it in on-screen text, and put it in the caption written the way people search.

### C. Series with a named constraint

Series-based posting is one of the defining 2026 formats. Subway Build is the strongest asset on this channel and it is under-shipped. A constraint format is a *format*, not a topic, which means it gives viewers a reason to return and gives the algorithm a series signal.

Tighten the constraint so it is legible in the first frame: 45 minutes, one train ride, no wifi past 14th Street, one shipped thing. Number the episodes.

### D. Receipts and failures

The documented counter-trend to AI saturation is authenticity and mess. Only 26% of consumers now prefer AI-generated creator content, down from 60% in 2023, and audiences actively scroll past anything that reads too polished.

Failure content is the one thing in this niche that cannot be faked or mass-produced. What broke, what it cost, what did not work. "I spent $340 in tokens and shipped nothing" outperforms "5 ways I use Claude" because it has stakes and a number.

### E. News reaction with a builder's edge

News pegs come with a search-demand spike attached. The differentiator here is not the take, it is that the take comes from someone who shipped on the thing that day. Speed plus first-hand POV.

## 4. Reframes: same knowledge, working shape

| Dead framing | Working framing |
|---|---|
| How I use AI at work | I replaced sprint planning with one MCP server. Here is what my PM job looks like now. |
| My AI workflow | My token bill was higher than my AWS bill this month. Here is what that actually buys. |
| 5 Claude Code tips | Claude Code cost me $340 this month. Full breakdown, and the 3 things that wasted most of it. |
| AI is changing engineering | We did not backfill the last two engineers. Nobody wants to say why out loud. |
| How to use MCP | What is MCP? Explained for PMs in 90 seconds. (keyword in first 2 sec, on screen, in caption) |
| Building with AI on my commute | Subway Build #7. 45 minutes, no wifi past 14th Street, one shipped feature. |
| AI tools for founders | I am a solo technical founder. Here is the exact headcount my stack replaces, with the math. |
| AI agents are getting better | I gave an agent my calendar for a week. Here is everything it got wrong. |
| Why I love Claude Code | Three things Claude Code still cannot do that everyone claims it can. |
| Vibe coding is the future | I shipped a product with vibe coding. Six weeks later I had to rewrite half of it. |

Pattern: every working framing has at least one of a number, a consequence, a named artifact, or an admission.

## 5. Packaging fixes independent of topic

- Longer short-form is favored. 60 to 180 seconds beats 25 seconds when watch time is a core ranking signal.
- Say the keyword out loud in the first two seconds. Repeat it on screen and in the caption.
- Write captions as queries, not as branding.
- Open on the consequence. The voice profile already says this, so check whether the actual scripts do it.
- 8 to 12 focused tags, no spam. Captions carry more discovery weight than hashtags now.

---

## 6. Going candid without losing retention

Candid is the right instinct and it matches the authenticity counter-trend, but the numbers come with a warning. Scripted video retains at roughly 40 to 60%, unscripted at 25 to 35%, and most of that gap opens in the first 30 seconds. Raw content reads as credible precisely because it is less filtered, and then dies on dead air, circular answers and missing context.

So the move is not "stop scripting." It is drop the performance, keep the structure.

### The three-beat note, not a script

Write three things on a note before you hit record. Nothing else.

1. **The opening line, verbatim.** The only sentence you memorize. It carries the whole retention curve.
2. **Two or three beats you want to hit.** Bullets, not sentences.
3. **The closing conviction, verbatim.** No question, per the voice profile.

Everything between beat one and beat three is live. That preserves the candid texture where it matters, in the middle, and protects the two moments where rambling actually costs views.

### Capture rules

- Record while doing the thing, not after. The reaction when something works or breaks is unrepeatable and un-fakeable, and it is the exact scarcity in an AI-slop feed.
- Keep a capture running during real work. Screen recording with live commentary, phone on the desk, voice memo on the platform. Harvest clips, do not stage them.
- Subway Build already is this format. The commute, the noise, the dead zone past 14th Street are the candid signal. Stop cleaning it up.
- One take with a hard constraint is itself a format promise, but only publish the takes that hold.

### Edit hard, this is where candid wins or loses

- Your published first frame should be roughly the eighth second of what you recorded. Cut the ramp-in.
- Never open on your face saying "so." Open on the artifact, the screen, the error, or the number.
- Cut every circular restatement. Candid earns you texture, not runtime.
- Good editing keeps the honesty and removes the friction. That is the whole job.

### Where candid fits, and where it does not

| Lane | Candid? |
|---|---|
| A. Consequence, AI x jobs x money | Yes. Conviction reads as fake when it is performed. |
| B. Search utility | No. Keyword-first packaging needs tight control. Keep these structured. |
| C. Subway Build | Yes. The constraint is the script. |
| D. Receipts and failures | Yes. This is the strongest candid fit on the channel. |
| E. News reaction | Yes, and speed matters more than polish here. |

---

## 7. 30-day test

Nine videos, three lanes, three each. Hold the format constant so the topic is the only variable.

- Lane A, consequence: AI x jobs, money, headcount, comp
- Lane B, search utility: three real queries, keyword-first packaging
- Lane C, receipts: one cost breakdown, one failure, one honest limitation

Measure at day 30 on median views and average completion, not on the single best video. Kill the worst lane, double the best, and keep Subway Build running underneath the whole test as the series spine.
